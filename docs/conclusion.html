Conclusion

Conclusion

The Secure QR-Based Attendance System shows that the common ways of cheating attendance can each be stopped by a specific, standard cryptographic mechanism: HMAC for authentic and fresh QR codes, ECDSA for binding scans to a student's own phone, nonces and timestamps against replay, a SHA-256 hash chain for tamper-evident records, Ed25519 for verifiable reports, HKDF for key separation and Argon2id for credentials.

The system was tested with unit and end-to-end tests (7 / 7 passing) and with a live attack suite in which all twelve attacks were blocked or detected. For the classroom, marking attendance takes a few seconds instead of several minutes of roll call, and defaulter lists and signed reports are produced automatically.

Key Takeaways

  • Most of the security lies in the protocol: what is signed, what is checked and in which order.
  • Defence in depth matters: the time window, nonce, device signature and uniqueness constraint each close a gap the others leave open.
  • Tamper evidence needs an external anchor; a hash chain alone can be recomputed, but a signed chain head cannot.
  • A clear threat model, including what the system does not stop, is part of a secure design.

Future Work

  • WebAuthn passkeys for hardware-backed device keys with biometric confirmation per scan.
  • Admin-approved device registration and one-device-per-phone enforcement.
  • Per-faculty signing keys for individual non-repudiation.
  • Integration with the university ERP and hosted deployment with a public TLS certificate.
